Three developments are changing how I think about the Indian IT outlook: possible higher H-1B costs, the risk that AI reduces some routine technology work, and the growing interest of Japanese companies in India-based GCCs.

I do not think any one of these supports a confident forecast. A higher H-1B cost could push more work offshore. It could also encourage U.S. hiring, GCC expansion or client insourcing. The direction is not automatic.

The AI question is similar. A report discussed by NDTV points to substantial concern about job reductions. I would not translate that into “20% of Indian IT jobs will disappear.” A more useful question is how much routine work is exposed, how quickly companies reduce fresher hiring, and whether reskilling creates enough higher-value work.

Japanese GCC expansion is a constructive counterweight. Japanese firms may bring engineering, digital and enterprise work to India. That could create opportunities beyond traditional outsourcing, but the actual benefit will depend on capability depth and execution.

I am adding these signals to my Indian IT Scenario Simulator as separate assumptions. The tool shows how my probability-weighted view changes when evidence changes. It is an MVP, and I am using it to test whether a transparent, continuously updated scenario approach is useful.

These are interpretations, not certainties. The simulator uses illustrative coefficients and should not be treated as investment or career advice.
